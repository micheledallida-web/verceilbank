6. Deposit Insurance

Deposit insurance protects the money customers place in deposit accounts at insured depository institutions in the event that the institution fails. It is provided by the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC), an independent agency of the United States government, and it is backed by the full faith and credit of the United States.

Standard maximum deposit insurance amount
$250,000
per depositor, per insured institution, for each account ownership category. Ownership categories are set by federal regulation and include, among others, single accounts, joint accounts, certain retirement accounts, and trust accounts. A customer holding deposits in more than one ownership category may therefore be insured for more than $250,000 in total at the same institution.

Coverage is calculated by the FDIC at the level of the depositor and the ownership category, not per account. Balances held across several deposit accounts in the same ownership category at the same institution are added together and insured to the single applicable limit rather than separately.

Not FDIC insured · May lose value · No bank guarantee

Investment and wealth management accounts, securities, market positions, digital assets and any other non-deposit product are not deposits, are not insured by the FDIC or any federal government agency, are not guaranteed by VerceilBank, and are subject to investment risk including the possible loss of the principal amount invested. Deposit insurance, where it applies, applies only to deposit balances.

Customers can determine their own coverage using the FDIC's Electronic Deposit Insurance Estimator at https://edie.fdic.gov, or by contacting VerceilBank through the support channels in clause 17.

7. Deposits and Funds Availability

Deposits are subject to verification and applicable funds availability policies.

VerceilBank may delay the availability of deposited funds when permitted by law, including for fraud prevention, regulatory review, deposit verification, security investigations, or operational reasons.

When deposited funds are available

For accounts subject to the federal funds availability rules (Regulation CC, 12 CFR Part 229), availability is determined from the business day of deposit. A business day is a weekday that is not a federal holiday, and a deposit made after the posted cut-off time, or on a day that is not a business day, is treated as received on the next business day.

Funds availability by deposit type
Type of deposit Generally available
Electronic deposits Direct deposits, incoming wire transfers and other electronic credits — the business day after the day the deposit is received.
Cash The business day after the day of deposit.
Specified check types U.S. Treasury checks, cashier's, certified and teller's checks, federal and state government checks, and checks drawn on VerceilBank — the business day after the day of deposit, where deposited to the payee's account.
Other checks The first $275 of the day's aggregate check deposits on the business day after deposit; the remainder generally by the second business day after deposit.

When a longer hold may apply

In the circumstances permitted by Regulation CC, availability may be delayed beyond the periods above:

  • New accounts, during the first 30 days after the account is opened
  • Aggregate check deposits exceeding $6,725 on any one business day
  • Checks that have been returned unpaid and are redeposited
  • Accounts that have been repeatedly overdrawn
  • Deposits VerceilBank has reasonable cause to believe will not be collectible
  • Emergency conditions, including failures of communications or computer equipment

For an account open 30 days or less, the first $6,725 of a day's deposits of U.S. Treasury checks, cashier's, certified and teller's checks and government checks is generally available on the business day after deposit, with the remainder generally available no later than the ninth business day.

Where availability is delayed under any of these exceptions, VerceilBank will tell the customer the reason and the day the funds will be available — at the time of deposit where the deposit is made in person, and otherwise by notice sent no later than the business day after the deposit is received.

The $275 and $6,725 figures are the thresholds set by the federal banking agencies with effect from July 1, 2025. They are adjusted for inflation every five years, and the amounts in force from time to time apply.

15. Error Resolution

Customers should report account errors or unauthorized transactions as soon as possible.

VerceilBank will investigate reported errors in accordance with applicable legal and regulatory requirements and will make appropriate corrections where warranted.

Electronic transfers on consumer accounts

For electronic fund transfers on consumer accounts, the federal Electronic Fund Transfer Act and Regulation E (12 CFR Part 1005) set the rules below. They apply to transfers such as card transactions, ATM withdrawals, direct deposits and other electronic debits and credits, and they cannot be reduced by agreement.

Reporting an unauthorized transfer, and what it can cost

Report a lost or stolen card, password or other access device, or any unauthorized transfer, as soon as it is discovered. How much a customer can be liable for depends almost entirely on how quickly they tell the Bank.

Consumer liability limits for unauthorized electronic fund transfers
When the customer notifies VerceilBank Maximum liability
Within 2 business days of learning of the loss or theft $50
More than 2 business days after learning of the loss or theft Up to $500
More than 60 days after a periodic statement showing an unauthorized transfer was sent Unlimited, for transfers occurring after that 60-day period

Where a delay in giving notice was caused by extenuating circumstances, such as extended travel or hospitalization, these periods will be extended by a reasonable time.

How to report

Notice may be given through any of the support channels in clause 17. To allow an investigation to begin, a report should give the customer's name and account number, describe the transfer in question, explain why it is believed to be an error, and state the dollar amount involved. Where notice is given orally, VerceilBank may require written confirmation within ten business days.

How the investigation runs

VerceilBank will determine whether an error occurred within ten business days of receiving the report, and will correct any error promptly. Where more time is needed, the investigation may take up to 45 days — but in that case the account will be provisionally credited with the amount in question within ten business days, so that the customer has use of the money while the review continues.

Longer periods apply in the cases the regulation allows: 20 business days to complete an investigation, and up to 90 days with provisional credit, for transfers on accounts open 30 days or less, for point-of-sale transactions, and for transfers initiated outside the United States.

Results will be reported within three business days of completing the investigation. If VerceilBank concludes that no error occurred, it will send a written explanation and may reverse any provisional credit; the customer may request copies of the documents relied on in reaching that conclusion.
